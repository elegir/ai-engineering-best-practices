# Data for AI products

## 1. The question this answers

Before a product answers or acts over documents and data: how do we know what is in the corpus and whether it can be trusted; how is each data type turned into something a reasoning model and an embedding model can use without losing structure or provenance; and what must never enter the index, or must be removable from it?

## 2. Short answer

**Audit first and re-audit on every parser, source or policy change**: counts by type, duplicates, empty or garbled pages, PII hits, date coverage, sources with no owner. **Contracts and gates decide what may be embedded, retrieved or acted on** — "gold data is not automatically AI approved data" (Gambill, 2026-06, `yt-tkFr-4r906Q…`). **Parse layout-first**: small models find the regions of a page, each region goes to the model it needs, the output is an **element-typed document with provenance** (page, bounding box, confidence), and views are derived per consumer — Markdown for text, HTML for a complex table, a natural-language rendering for the embedding model. **The frontier VLM is a tool called on a hard region, not the parser of a million pages.** **Evaluate the parser on thousands of your own pages**, and every stage, not only the final answer. **Privacy is three placements**: scrub PII before anything is embedded, indexed or cached; filter by identity in the store — Row Level Security when the store is Postgres; redact at output as the last net. **Know whether data is anonymised or pseudonymised**; a reversible placeholder keeps linkage and is the second. **Write owner, tenant and sensitivity at ingestion**, by the ingestion identity. **Prove that deletion reaches the index, the caches, the eval sets and the logs.** Files: `practices/data-ingestion/`.

## 3. Long explanation

### 3.1 Why data is the bottleneck

Every parsing source says it from its own seat. Adit Abraham (Reducto, AI Engineer 2026-09, `yt-0I07YAuF8xA…`): the move "to actual agents doing work" changes the cost of a bad input — an agent "making multiple decisions that compound" propagates it; "you don't necessarily know what is in your corpus of information" — the definition of the audit problem. Reducto on Jason Liu's channel (2026-01, `yt-ybzR4LBY0Lo…`): "better document parsing always gives you better downstream task accuracy". IBM Technology on Docling (2025-08, `yt-zSA7ylHP6AY…`): "the real challenge… isn't building the agent, but curating the knowledge". Jerry Liu at Data+AI Summit (2024-07, `yt-dI_TmTW9S4c…`): "garbage in, garbage out". Barnett et al. (`barnett-seven-failure-points-abstract.md`, 2024-01): "validation of a RAG system is only feasible during operation" — the production sample of `14-evals-and-error-analysis.md` §3.3 applied to ingestion. Until this module the KB deferred all of this to "the RAG sessions" (`11-runtime-context-management.md` §3.4).

### 3.2 Audit and the control plane

**Data-centric doctrine (Andrew Ng, Data+AI Summit 2022-07, `yt-avoijDORAlc…`; supervised vision, carried as doctrine).** "Data centric AI is the discipline of systematically engineering the data": hold the model fixed, iterate on the data. A good dataset has "consistent and accurate labels", representative inputs, and reflects "post deployment changes". Three mechanisms transfer: (1) inconsistent labels mean "I didn't write clear enough labeling instructions" — the team-edited **label book** has "some of the highest ROI", the 2022 origin of the s5 rule that annotator disagreement means an unclear rubric (`14` §3.1); (2) **agreement-based labeling** to surface "where exactly are the disagreements", then slicing to the problem subset; (3) cleaning is "a core part of the iterative process", not a step done once — re-run the audit after every parser or source change, let trace errors decide what is re-parsed.

**The control plane (Chris Gambill, 2026-06, `yt-tkFr-4r906Q…`; a practitioner selling a checklist — mechanisms kept).** "The data plane executes work. The control plane decides what work is actually allowed to happen." A **data contract** "includes shape, meaning, ownership, freshness, compatibility rules, and what happens when the contract breaks". **Lineage** answers "what else is now contaminated?" — otherwise debugging "becomes archaeology with Slack messages". Four gates in the data plane (source contract, schema and meaning, quality, publish) and a fifth, the **AI approval gate**: "gold data is not automatically AI approved data… can this data set be embedded? Can it be retrieved by a model? Can an agent act on it?" Because "bad data in a RAG system can become a confident answer to a customer… in an agent workflow [it] can trigger an action." His landing-zone minimum — identify the source, detect schema breakage, quarantine malformed records, "tag sensitivity and region" — is the field list of the audit report and the envelope in the practice. Two more rules: decide "which sources are authoritative for which business concepts" first; set freshness by "what business decision gets worse if the data is 5 minutes old?". Checklist: `practices/data-ingestion/audit-checklist.md`.

**The audit in the vendors' practice.** Reducto's **classify and split** (`yt-0I07YAuF8xA…`): packets "can be hundreds of pages long", so route each document or section to its pipeline before extraction — over-stuffed context erodes quality. The evaluation rule every vendor agrees on (`yt-ybzR4LBY0Lo…`): five documents are "a very high variance estimate"; the alternative is an automated comparison over "thousands of examples" from the corpus. Abraham: "eval should underpin all of your decisions", **at every stage**, parsing included. Dave Ebbelaar (2025-02, `yt-9lBTS5dM27c…`) shows the minimal envelope: filename, page numbers and title beside every chunk.

### 3.3 Parsing — why PDFs are hard, and the pipeline that reads them

**The format.** Liu at AI Dev 26 (2026-05, `yt-80vV6fGIlWo…`): a PDF "is a bunch of machine instructions that help like print characters"; a table is "lines, borders, and then also values with coordinate positions" with no association; reading order in a multi-column page "could be totally arbitrary". Abraham: humans "encode so much context visually". Reducto's cases (`yt-ybzR4LBY0Lo…`): circled options, handwriting across a divider, a watermarked table — "you cannot write custom prompts for everything".

**Three generations.** Heuristic pipelines (PyPDF, PyMuPDF, pdftotext, Tesseract) cluster characters by spacing: "the moment like the document format deviates… it's going to break" (Liu 2026); more "information chaos and loss" in the data, higher "likelihood of some sort of hallucination" downstream (Liu 2024). Trained document models (Donut) one-shot a distribution. VLMs read like a human and handle the long tail, but "we don't think that they're a one-sizefits-all solution" (Abraham, `yt-0I07YAuF8xA…`; §3.4).

**The pipeline shape** (Reducto: "a blended approach. We divide and conquer", `yt-ybzR4LBY0Lo…`; Docling, `docling-technical-report-abstract.md`). (1) **Layout first.** Abraham: small models "are really effective for… detecting the layout of a document… can actually run on CPU"; Docling names **DocLayNet** for layout and **TableFormer** for table structure, "on commodity hardware in a small resource budget". (2) **Route by region**: OCR, layout, table-extraction and key-value models chosen by complexity, length and size; expensive autoregressive models "are only needed for the most challenging cases". (3) **Grounding is the product**: "we basically have a pixel map for every single element"; extraction returns citations with confidences, and a missing field is cited "in the document that it was missing". Liu: "at least like region-level or line-level citations", which "doesn't come out of the box if you just call the VLM API". Docling: "every element includes provenance, page and bounding box information" (`yt-zSA7ylHP6AY…`). Citations in `13-structured-outputs-and-guardrails.md` §3.2 and `21-agent-design-and-tools.md` §3.6 gain page and bounding box. (4) **Agentic OCR — verification, never rewriting** (Abraham): token-level edits ("a period versus a comma, a zero versus an O"), not "sending OCR to Gemini… asking it nicely to not deviate too much", because a rewriting model that sees "total" will "add up the values in the table themselves". (5) **Use the file's metadata when it is good**: Reducto's "hybrid extraction mode… will look at the metadata and say like is this actually good". (6) **One view per consumer** (§3.5).

**Benchmarks.** OmniDocBench "is starting to become a little bit saturated"; olmOCR-bench is "academic papers… binary pass fail" (Liu 2026). ParseBench and RD-TableBench are vendor leaderboards. **Rule (opinion):** a public benchmark shortlists; your own thousand pages decide. Dated names, prices and positions: `practices/data-ingestion/parser-selection-notes.md`.

### 3.4 Frontier VLMs as parsers

Four failure modes from two vendors who compete with the models they describe — mechanisms to *test for*, not rankings (digest §4): **silent drops** of content (precision high, recall low — Abraham, `yt-0I07YAuF8xA…`), **repetition at low temperature** and the hallucination trade-off when it is raised (Reducto, `yt-ybzR4LBY0Lo…`), **thinking that does not improve visual accuracy** and **a per-page cost that does not scale to a million PDFs** (Liu, `yt-80vV6fGIlWo…`); quotes, model names and prices as read on 2026-10-01 in `practices/data-ingestion/parser-selection-notes.md` §4. For long documents, Reducto's rule: "always parse them and then work with a parsed text", whereas "if your input is like a 100 images… hallucinations are everywhere".

**Where the VLM belongs: in the loop, on demand.** Liu on the model makers' assistants: text parsing first, then "screenshot the page and then feed it into itself" — fine for assistive work, not for a corpus. A model-free first pass that keeps bounding boxes (his LightParse, `yt-80vV6fGIlWo…`), then — at AI Engineer four months later — "equip a VLM-based parser like Llama Parser or other frontier models as a tool" (2026-09, `yt-RQi7x-navxU…`, s2 reused). Abraham's customers "create effectively a file system for their agent to go through and navigate… [with] all the metadata" — `11` §3.2 *Offload* with parsed documents as the files. **Position (digest §5):** local, model-free parser plus a frontier VLM as a tool on hard regions; a vendor when your own thousand-page audit shows the long tail is yours. Vision-RAG — embedding the page image and handing it to a VLM — is the retrieval-side form of the same rule: pixels where the parse breaks, at 10–20× the tokens and more hallucination (Reimers, 2025-05; `17-embeddings-and-chunking.md` §3.8).

### 3.5 Architecture by data type — one document model, then a view per consumer

**One representation for every input.** Docling's `DoclingDocument` is hierarchical "with element types, headings, and per-element metadata" (`yt-zSA7ylHP6AY…`), exported as Markdown, HTML, DocTags or "lossless JSON" (`docling-readme.md`). Unstructured's **partitioning** yields elements for "paragraphs, tables, images, code snippets" (`unstructured-chunking-for-rag-best-practices.md`, 2024-07-17); its chunking page (`unstructured-chunking-concepts.md`, read 2026-10-01) keeps a `Table` element that "is not combined with other elements", with `text_as_html`, `page_number` / `filename` metadata and `orig_elements` for traceability. LlamaIndex's `Document` → `Node`s, Readers (a `DatabaseReader` that "returns every row of the results as a Document") and `IngestionPipeline` (`llamaindex-loading-ingestion.md`) name the stages all agree on: **load → transform → index/store**. The reference `Element` in `practices/data-ingestion/ingestion_pipeline.py` is this representation reduced to six types with page, bounding box and confidence.

**By type** — table in `practices/data-ingestion/architecture-by-data-type.md`; the rules in one paragraph. *PDFs and scans*: §3.3; "page level chunking… is honestly a pretty strong baseline" (Liu 2024; confirmed by Reimers 2025 — chunking is `17-embeddings-and-chunking.md` §3.2). *Tables*: a simple table in Markdown, "a more complex table… something like HTML", because merged cells "encode a lot of meaning" at the cost of tokens; and a **second rendering for the embedding model** — embedding models "really struggle" with "this messy blob of HTML tags and numbers", so build "a natural language representation of that table", embed that, pass the HTML to the reasoning model (Abraham; Liu 2024's "embed… an extracted summary, or embed the caption" is the same move). Routing simple-vs-complex was "a user controlled thing" in 2026-01, "a dynamic problem" by 2026-09. *HTML and sites*: Docling parses the HTML directly; whole sites via `sitemap.xml` (Ebbelaar). *Images and charts*: Docling can "enrich figures with text descriptions so that they're retrievable alongside text" (`yt-zSA7ylHP6AY…`); its README lists chart understanding converted "into tables or code" (`docling-readme.md`). *Databases*: text-to-SQL with "an intermediate step to predict what tables to use" when the schema is large (`huyen-genai-platform.md`), versus rows-as-documents; `practices/context-management/context-store-decision.md` q7. *Spreadsheets, audio, video*: supported by the parsers; no source says more.

**Chunks or the tree** (IBM Technology, 2026-08, `yt-vRZNJWw78BQ…`). "The author already organized the information for you. Chunking takes that tree and flattens it… and then spend a lot more effort trying to recover the relationships." The alternative keeps the parsed tree, gives the agent an outline with a summary per section and tools to open one — "this is still retrieval. It just keeps the document whole". Costs, same video: "you need a good tree before any of it works… That's most of the engineering"; "more latency and more calls"; at "millions of documents… similarity search is hard to beat" — the exact-search threshold of `18-vector-stores.md` §3.1 is where that begins (s8). The resolution — "similarity search to find the right document, structure to navigate inside of it" — is a new row in `context-store-decision.md` and the document form of `21` §3.6. How the tree is cut into chunks when similarity search is the answer — tables standalone, headings attached, the model's tokens — is `17-embeddings-and-chunking.md` §3.2 (s7).

### 3.6 Privacy and compliance — PII before the index, reversible redaction, the anonymity test, deletion proof

**Where the PII check goes.** Gambill: "PII filtering must happen before sensitive data gets embedded, indexed, cached, copied, or scattered like glitter". Chip Huyen (`huyen-genai-platform.md`, 2024-07-25): a leak is "prevented by… not allowing it to retrieve sensitive data in the first place"; her **input guardrail**: detect classes "specified by you", then "mask a user's phone number with the placeholder [PHONE NUMBER]… use a PII reversible dictionary… so that you can unmask it". Katharine Jarmul (GOTO 2025 talk, uploaded 2026-01, `yt-lz0L0rRV7RE…`) shows why retrieval-time filtering alone is late: an early ChatGPT app returned "the file with everybody's salaries". **Three placements, all three (position, digest §5):** scrub at ingestion what must never enter the index (`practices/structured-outputs/guardrail-policy.md` G0, new); filter at retrieval what some identities may see (§3.7, G6); redact at output as the last net (G3). Ingestion is the only one cheap to prove.

**The detector and its warning.** Presidio (`presidio-readme.md`, read 2026-10-01; MIT): "predefined or custom PII recognizers leveraging Named Entity Recognition, regular expressions, rule based logic and checksum" — and the sentence every policy document carries: "there is no guarantee that Presidio will find all sensitive information". The tiers of `13` §3.3 apply: checksum and regex first, NER second, a classifier or judge last.

**What anonymous means** (EDPB press text, 2026-07-08, `edpb-anonymisation-web-scraping-genai.md`; the guidelines are the s6 validation read). "Data is anonymous if it does not relate to an identified or identifiable natural person", which "may vary from one entity to another"; the test is **three criteria — no record isolation, no linkage, no inference** — by a "contextual approach" or a "simplified approach" that may treat data "as though it is not anonymous even if it would actually be so" but gives "greater confidence". A placeholder whose dictionary survives fails *linkage*: Huyen's masking is pseudonymisation, not anonymisation (this KB's reading; the page uses the word only as a tag). **Scraping for AI**: GDPR applies to "collection, storage, organisation and retrieval"; "reliable sources, recording the timestamp, and validating the data"; data minimisation; special categories "in principle prohibited". Consultation until 2026-10-30 — re-read after.

**Deletion and lineage.** Gambill: "can you prove that this customer's data was actually deleted…? Did the vector index retain anything?" — caches, eval sets and logs follow — "test deletion like you're testing your recovery"; eval datasets "need lineage and quality controls. Otherwise, your evaluation numbers become just acting theater" (`practices/evals/eval-policy.md`). IBM (`yt-kyJ1vd7yEPc…`, 2026-09): data "gets transformed by RAG", so track "child files… derived from" a sensitive file. The erasure path of `15-memory-external-context-and-permissions.md` §3.2 therefore names four places; and because a deleted vector is hidden in the graph before it is gone (Dilocker 2023; pgvector's vacuum note — `18` §3.2), the deletion test *queries* the index, it does not count rows. Jarmul for the threat model: nothing in a prompt "you wouldn't want to write on your public website"; poisoning "of other documents that are on your infrastructure" is "more meaningful to think about" than training-data poisoning (T14); an unauthenticated public interface will be exploited (T20); "use guardrails but they're not going to save you". Docling's "local execution… for sensitive data and air-gapped environments" (`docling-readme.md`) is the parser-side form of that choice (T7).

### 3.7 Tenant isolation at the data layer — RLS, not a WHERE clause

Supabase (`supabase-rag-with-permissions.md`, read 2026-10-01): with pgvector on Postgres "you can implement fine-grained access control on your vector database using Row Level Security (RLS)" — an owner column on the documents table, a policy on the sections, and "semantic search over these sections (or any additional filtering for that matter) will continue to respect these RLS policies"; the table and policy shape, and the variants (join table, foreign data wrapper, session variable, JWT claim), are in `practices/data-ingestion/privacy-compliance-checklist.md` §4. The sentence that settles the s5 placement question for Postgres: "we recommend RLS as a general best practice since RLS is always applied even as new queries and application logic is introduced in the future." Gambill: "if a user can't access a customer record directly, they should not be able to ask a chatbot to summarize it through the side door… access control has to flow into this retrieval" — `15` §3.5's rule. **Position (digest §5):** Pinecone/AuthZed's pre/post-filter is the *mechanism* when authorisation lives outside the store; RLS or the store's native partition is the *enforcement point* when the store is the database; an application filter is defence in depth, never the control; the two-identity test proves it either way. **Refined 2026-10-05 (s8):** on an approximate index the policy is applied *after* the scan — "sharing an approximate index between tenants means vectors from one tenant can affect recall (and speed) for other tenants. For tenant isolation, use list partitioning or separate tables" (pgvector README, read 2026-10-05) — so RLS keeps a partition, a partial index or `hnsw.iterative_scan` beside it, and the two-tenant proof runs with the approximate index present at the production `ef_search` and asserts *k* results per tenant (`18-vector-stores.md` §3.7; `practices/vector-store/tenant-isolation-in-the-store.md`). The owner column is written at ingestion (practice Verify 2), closing half of the s5 park; chunk-level inheritance from shared sources stays with session 10.

## 4. How to apply it in a repo

The Verify of `practices/data-ingestion/README.md`, in order; the practice is `draft` and **unrouted** until the routing gate of `decisions/0004-day-one-for-blank-and-existing-repos.md` §6.

1. Confirm `retrieval` (`practices/facts.md`). Generate the audit report for every corpus (`ingestion_pipeline.py --audit`) before any index exists; record the authoritative source per concept and the owner and sensitivity per source in `audit-checklist.md`.
2. Put every parser behind the `Parser` adapter so the store sees only typed elements with page, bounding box and confidence; keep tables as table elements with an HTML or JSON view and a text rendering for the embedding model (`architecture-by-data-type.md`).
3. Write the envelope on every element — source id, filename, page, type, content hash, owner and tenant from the ingestion identity, sensitivity, region, ingested-at, parser version — and refuse an element without owner or page.
4. Choose the parser on at least a thousand of the product's own pages with an automated comparison; track it on a production sample (`parser-selection-notes.md`).
5. Make ingestion idempotent by document hash: a second run adds nothing; a changed document replaces; a removed one is tombstoned.
6. Full part (`personal_data or regulated or multi_tenant`): scrub or quarantine PII classes and secret shapes before the index, any reversible dictionary outside it; test the erasure path over index, caches, eval sets and logs; prove tenant isolation with two tenants and overlapping content, as an RLS policy on Postgres; state in `privacy-compliance-checklist.md` anonymised vs pseudonymised and the regimes in scope.

## 5. Anti-patterns

- An index built before anyone counted the documents; an audit report older than the parser.
- A parser chosen on five documents; a leaderboard quoted instead of the product's own pages.
- A frontier VLM parsing the whole corpus; a model asked to "clean up" OCR and recomputing a total.
- Tables flattened to prose; the HTML of a table fed to the embedding model.
- Chunks with no page, source or owner; an owner written from the request that triggered a re-index.
- PII scrubbed only at output; a placeholder dictionary beside the embeddings; "anonymised" claimed for linkable data.
- A tenant filter that exists only in a `WHERE` clause of application code.
- An erasure that deletes the row and leaves the embedding, the cached answer, the eval case or the log line.
