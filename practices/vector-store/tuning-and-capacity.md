## 1. HNSW parameters — one meaning, four vendors' defaults

| Parameter | What it trades | pgvector (`pgvector-readme.md`) | Qdrant (`qdrant-course-what-is-hnsw.md`) | Weaviate (`weaviate-vector-index-concepts.md`) | Pinecone Faiss chapter (`pinecone-faiss-hnsw.md`) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `M` (edges per node) | memory and recall up; build slower | `m` "the max number of connections per layer (16 by default)" | `m` 8 / **16** / 32 in its fast / balanced / accurate presets; "between 8 and 64" | `maxConnections` | memory depends on `M` alone: "Both efConstruction and efSearch do not affect index memory usage, leaving us only with M"; `M_max0 set to M*2`; level multiplier `1/ln(M)` |
| `ef_construction` (build candidate list) | graph quality up; build slower | "the size of the dynamic candidate list for constructing the graph (64 by default)" | `ef_construct` 100 / 200 / 400 in its presets; the page marks **both** 100 and 200 as "default" in different blocks (digest §4: re-read the configuration reference at the validation pass) | `efConstruction` | — |
| `ef` (query candidate list) | recall up; latency up; **caps the result count** | `hnsw.ef_search` "40 by default"; "Results are limited by the size of the dynamic candidate list" | `hnsw_ef` 32 / **128** / 256, set at search time | dynamic: `ef = min(max(dynamicEfMin, queryLimit * dynamicEfFactor), dynamicEfMax)` | Sift1M: recall "from 80%-1ms to 100%-50ms" across settings |
| exact-search threshold | no index below it | — (no index = exact) | `full_scan_threshold` 10000 (a vector count in the course; "10 kilobytes" in the filtering guide) | dynamic index switches flat → HNSW at "by default 10,000" objects | — |

**Rule (pgvector README, the last line of its tuning list): "use the defaults unless seeing low recall."** Katz's recommendation of `ef_construction` 256 (PGConf.EU 2024-11) is a speaker's opinion against the default of 64 and is recorded as such. Katz on `M`: "the most important parameter in terms of the quality of the index" (Postgres.FM, 2024-01-19) and "16 is kind of like a magic number" (PGConf.EU, 2024-11-04). A change of any value is a new `IndexSpec` and needs its own benchmark record (`vector_store.py`, README Verify 3).

This repo: `m=<<M>>`, `ef_construction=<<EF_CONSTRUCTION>>`, `ef_search=<<EF_SEARCH>>` — or `lists=<<LISTS>>`, `probes=<<PROBES>>` — with the benchmark record path beside each value.

## 2. Build, deletes, vacuum

- **Cost.** An insert "is approximately two times more expensive than just searching", "very CPU intensified" (Vasnetsov 2023); a million points "can easily run for an hour on a pretty beefy machine" (Liberty 2021). pgvector: "Indexes build significantly faster when the graph fits into `maintenance_work_mem`" (its example sets 8 GB; the server warns `hnsw graph no longer fits into maintenance_work_mem after 100000 tuples`); `max_parallel_maintenance_workers` (example 7 "plus leader"; `max_parallel_workers` "8 by default"); load with `COPY … (FORMAT BINARY)` and "create an index after loading your initial data"; `CREATE INDEX CONCURRENTLY` on a live table.
- **Inputs that ruin a graph.** Random vectors make "a horrible graph" (Dilocker 2023; Weaviate's benchmark FAQ agrees); duplicates stall the early-exit condition (Weaviate docs). Never benchmark on synthetic vectors (`benchmark-protocol.md`).
- **Deletes.** "hnsw actually doesn't support a notion of deletion… you first hide it and then you repair the graph" (Katz 2024-11); Weaviate tombstones and rebuilds at around 30 % tombstones or repairs in the background (Dilocker 2023); pgvector: "Vacuuming can take a while for HNSW indexes. Speed it up by reindexing first" (`REINDEX INDEX CONCURRENTLY`, then `VACUUM`). The erasure proof of `../data-ingestion/` Verify 7 therefore **queries** the index at the production parameters; it never counts rows.
- **IVFFlat** (pgvector): lists "`rows / 1000` for up to 1M rows and `sqrt(rows)` for over 1M rows", probes "`sqrt(lists)`" as "a good place to start"; k-means on a sample — "no matter the size of your data… you'll Sample about 10,000 vectors when you build your cluster" (Katz, PGConf.EU 2024-11-04); "Create the index after the table has some data"; centres skew under inserts — rebuild.

## 3. Filters — the planner, the iterative scan, the payload index

- **pgvector 0.8.0** (`pgvector-readme.md`, `pgvector-080-release.md`, 2024-11-11): "With approximate indexes, filtering is applied *after* the index is scanned. If a condition matches 10 % of rows, with HNSW and the default `hnsw.ef_search` of 40, only 4 rows will match on average." (The sentence lives here and in `tenant-isolation-in-the-store.md` §1 only.) `SET hnsw.iterative_scan = strict_order | relaxed_order` (also `ivfflat.iterative_scan = relaxed_order`) scans "until enough results are found", bounded by `hnsw.max_scan_tuples` (20000 in the README's example; `ivfflat.max_probes` for IVF); the planner may now "select a B-tree or other index" when the filter is selective. "If filtering by only a few distinct values, consider partial indexing… many different values, consider partitioning." Both settings are **per transaction** (`SET LOCAL`): a pooled connection without a transaction leaks them.
- **Qdrant** (`qdrant-course-filterable-hnsw.md`, `qdrant-vector-search-filtering.md`): a payload index is declared per field *before* the load so HNSW can "build filter-aware links"; the planner picks per segment by cardinality — graph walk for broad filters, payload-index pre-filter for narrow ones, full scan below the threshold; `is_tenant: true` on the tenant key co-locates a tenant's vectors.
- **Weaviate** (Dilocker 2025-02): roaring bitmaps in the LSM store and a bit-sliced index for ranges — "you never have to merge more than 64 bit maps"; the correlated-filter case handled with "a variation of acorn".
- **ClickHouse** (2026-01-28): `vector_search_filter_strategy` overrides the heuristic that decides pre- vs post-filtering.
- **The record** this repo keeps: one row per filter class in the store manifest — name, strategy (`in_graph | pre | iterative | exact`) and the path of the filtered benchmark record that measured its selectivity (matching rows / rows on the exact backend); a typed selectivity is refused, and `post` on a class at or under `SELECTIVE_BELOW` is refused by `vector_store.py` (README Verify 5).

## 4. Segments, memory tiers and the three recipes (Qdrant), the vector cache (Weaviate)

- **Latency vs throughput** (Vasnetsov 2023; `qdrant-optimize-performance.md`): one segment per core for latency; "fewer segments (usually 2) of larger size" for throughput.
- **Memory tiers** (`qdrant-optimize-performance.md`, v1.19 vocabulary): `memory: pinned` (RAM), `cached`, `cold` (disk via memmap, speed "depends on the disk's IOPS"); `inline_storage` for a cold index "roughly 3–6 times" larger. **Three recipes** the page gives: (1) quantized vectors `pinned` in RAM, originals `cold` on disk; (2) vectors and HNSW both `cold` with a denser graph (`m` 64, `ef_construct` 512) because disk reads, not CPU, bound it; (3) everything in RAM with quantization and rescoring. Qdrant's RAM sentence (`qdrant-resource-optimization.md`): "storing 1 million vectors with 1024 dimensions would require approximately 5.72 GB of RAM" — that is vectors × dimensions × 4 bytes with no graph overhead stated; the ×1.5 rule of thumb sometimes quoted for "vectors + index" is **not** in the snapshot and is not carried.
- **Weaviate** (`weaviate-vector-index-concepts.md`): set `vectorCacheMaxObjects` "high enough that all vectors can be held in memory" during import — "If the cache fills, Weaviate drops the whole cache"; async indexing on "a persistent, on-disk queue" (v1.28).
- **Postgres storage** (Katz 2024-01; README): a vector over about 2 kilobytes ("around like 514 dimensions") is TOASTed out of line and "can screw up the query planner"; `SET STORAGE PLAIN` costs a full 8 KB page per 1,536-dimensional vector; index pages cap `vector` at 2,000 dimensions, `halfvec` at 4,000, `bit` at 64,000 (README) — `halfvec` is the README's own suggestion for "a smaller working set".

## 5. Quantization inside the store — per vendor, with the originals' placement and reversibility (README Verify 6)

| Method | Compression and claims (vendor, read 2026-10-05) | Needs | Originals and reversibility |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scalar int8** (`sq8`) | Qdrant: "a 75 % reduction in memory", "the go-to choice for most use cases… error rates remain below 1 %"; Weaviate: "a 4x reduction", training set "100,000 objects per shard" by default | rescoring recommended | Qdrant keeps the originals (`always_ram` for the quantized copy; "Without explicitly setting on disk to true, you won't see any RAM savings"); Weaviate: "once set on a collection, quantization can't be disabled" |
| **Binary** (`bq1`) | Qdrant: "32x" memory, "up to 40x" speed, Hamming distance "in just two CPU instructions" (Vasnetsov 2023), for "models that have at least 1,024 dimensions"; Weaviate: "encouraging recall with Cohere's V3 models… and OpenAI's ada-002… test BQ with your own data"; pgvector: expression index `binary_quantize(embedding)::bit(n)` with `bit_hamming_ops`, then "Re-rank by the original vectors for better recall" (inner `LIMIT 20`, outer `LIMIT 5`); Katz 2024-11: "33,000 queries per second… but it's only 80 % recall whereas the others… about 93 %" | **always** oversampling and rescoring (Qdrant: disabling rescore is "only applicable if using binary quantization or TurboQuant with 1, 1.5 and 2 bits") | as above per store; pgvector keeps the `vector` column |
| **Product** (`pq`) | Dilocker 2023: codebooks of "256 options" per segment, "six or eight [dimensions per segment] works really great on something like OpenAI embeddings", 24× at 95 % recall for "20 % or so throughput" on his SIFT chart once over-fetch and rescore are on; Weaviate: segments "must divide evenly the original vector dimension", training "10,000 to 100,000 records per shard", random sample since v1.27; Qdrant: 4–64×, "we don't usually recommend due to the high accuracy loss" | representative training sample (never the first N "in order"); rescoring | Weaviate irreversible per collection |
| **Rotational** (`rq8`, `rq4`, `rq1`, Weaviate) | "no training phase"; 8-bit "4x compression while maintaining 98-99 % recall in internal testing" — "you can often disable rescoring"; 4-bit (preview v1.39) "94-95 %… rescoring the top 50… 99.8 % or better"; 1-bit asymmetric "essentially matches BQ recall" and "works well… where BQ performs poorly"; "inspired by extended RaBitQ" | `rescoreLimit` | irreversible per collection |

turbopuffer stores what you send: "I have to store that in full precision cuz you want to be able to export that later… we don't support like int8 or binary vectors, but we will" (Eskildsen, hosted by Jason Liu, 2025-11-04, `yt-l2N4DT35PKg-…`). Qdrant's `turbo4` and `turboquant` (v1.19) are recorded by name only.

This repo: `<<QUANTIZATION>>` with originals `<<ORIGINALS>>`; before/after recall copied by `StoreManifest.set_quantization()` from two benchmark records; an irreversible store gets the before/after **first**. A store-quantization, index-type, parameter or placement change is a **rebuild from the stored vectors under a new store manifest, never a re-embed** (`../embeddings-and-chunking/embedding-config-and-versioning.md` §6).

## 6. Capacity — the formula and the vendors' arithmetic (README Verify 7)

```
bytes = vectors × dimensions × bytes_per_value(dtype)          # float32 4, bfloat16/float16 2, int8 1, binary 1/8
      + vectors × M × 2 × GRAPH_EDGE_BYTES                      # graph; GRAPH_EDGE_BYTES is an APPROXIMATION (8; see vector_store.py)
      + quantized copy (when the originals are kept)
      × replicas (<<REPLICAS>>)
```

The vendors' versions, read 2026-10-05: pgvector — `4 * dimensions + 8` bytes per `vector`, `2 * dimensions + 8` per `halfvec`, `dimensions / 8 + 8` per `bit`; Qdrant — "storing 1 million vectors with 1024 dimensions would require approximately 5.72 GB of RAM" (`qdrant-resource-optimization.md`; vectors only, no graph term); Weaviate's table (s7 snapshot `weaviate-late-chunking.md`) — vectors "4B (float) x N dimensions" per object, edges "10B x 20 connections" per node. Dilocker's arithmetic (2023): 1,536 float32 dimensions "6 kilobytes… a million of them… 6 gigabytes… a billion… terabytes". `vector_store.py`'s `capacity_estimate()` recomputes this from the manifest's counts and refuses an in-RAM placement above `<<AVAILABLE_MEMORY_BYTES>>`. The estimate is a budget line of `../security-baseline/threat-model-agentic.md` T20.

**Napkin math first** (Eskildsen, CMU 2026-03-10, `yt-pqoRNwNaxfs-…`): "construct a model, very simple model of the system… if that gap is off by an order of magnitude… either there's a massive opportunity to improve the system or there's a bug in your understanding" — "This always beats profiling."

## 7. When to re-benchmark, and the embedding call in the budget

Re-run `benchmark-protocol.md` after every change of index type, parameter, quantization or placement, and when the corpus crosses a size band of `index-selection-table.md`; the production monitor (`sampled_recall()`, README Verify 9) runs on a schedule in between. **The embedding call is inside the latency budget** (Eskildsen, hosted by Jason Liu, 2025-11-04; his figures live in `store-selection.md` §2 only) — the measured P50 goes into `store-selection.md` §2 and is a selection criterion of `../embeddings-and-chunking/embedding-model-selection.md` §1.
